## 3. Visiting the website (hosting on GitHub Pages) {#hosting}

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. GitHub is part of Microsoft Corporation.

**What data?** So that your browser can display the pages, it automatically sends information to the server with every request: your IP address, date and time, the page or file requested, the page you came from (referrer), and the type and version of your browser and operating system. GitHub records this information in server logs.

**What for?** To deliver the website and to keep it running securely, for example against attacks intended to overload the server.

**Legal basis:** Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. I have a legitimate interest in my website being available, stable and secure.

**Retention:** The logs are stored only by GitHub. I cannot view them and do not analyse them. GitHub decides how long they are kept.

**USA:** GitHub also processes the data in the USA. According to its own information, GitHub participates in the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. For companies certified under it, the EU Commission has found that an adequate level of data protection exists (Art. 45 GDPR).

More information: [GitHub Privacy Statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement)

## 4. Contact form (Static Forms) {#contact-form}

**What data?** Whatever you enter in the form (name, email address, message). When you send it, technical details are also generated, such as your IP address, browser identifier and the page from which the form was sent.

**What for?** Solely to read and reply to your message.

**Legal basis:** Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. My legitimate interest is being able to reply to enquiries.

**Who is involved?** The form is handled by the Static Forms service (operator: Qualascend). Static Forms receives your input and sends it to me by email. Static Forms acts on my behalf; a data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR is part of the service's terms.

**Where and for how long?**

- Your form input is stored in an Amazon Web Services data centre in Frankfurt am Main, Germany, and deleted there automatically after 30 days.
- Static Forms itself runs on servers in the USA (provider: Vercel, Inc.). Technical logs of the transmission, i.e. IP address, browser identifier, originating page, field names and delivery status, but not the content of your message, are stored in the USA and, according to Static Forms, deleted after one year at the latest.
- The notification email containing your message is sent to me via Amazon Web Services in the USA.
- To detect spam, the CleanTalk service checks the input; this check takes place in the EU.

**USA:** Transfers to the USA are covered by the EU Commission's standard contractual clauses (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR), which are part of the Static Forms data processing agreement. Some of the providers used by Static Forms are additionally certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

**On my side:** Once your matter has been dealt with, I delete the message from my mailbox.

More information: [Static Forms privacy policy](https://www.staticforms.dev/privacy-policy) · [Data processing agreement](https://www.staticforms.dev/dpa)

## 6. Light or dark: your design setting {#design}

At the top right of every page there is a switch for light and dark design. Only if you click it does your browser remember your choice in its so-called local storage, so the page looks the same on your next visit. All that is stored is the word "light" or "dark". It stays on your device and is not sent to me or anyone else.

Your consent is not required for this: under § 25(2) No. 2 of the German TDDDG (Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act), a website may store information on your device if this is strictly necessary for a function you have explicitly chosen to use. You can remove the entry at any time in your browser settings by deleting the stored data for this website.

All pages are transmitted encrypted via HTTPS (TLS). You can tell by the padlock symbol or "https://" in your browser's address bar. Beyond that, my main aim is to let as little data as possible arise in the first place.

## 9. Your rights {#rights}

You can ask me at any time for:

- **access**: whether and what data I process about you, including a copy (Art. 15 GDPR),
- **rectification** of incorrect or incomplete data (Art. 16 GDPR),
- **erasure** of your data (Art. 17 GDPR),
- **restriction** of processing (Art. 18 GDPR),
- **a copy of the data you gave me** in a common, machine-readable format (Art. 20 GDPR).

An informal email to the address in section 1 is enough.

You can also lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR), for example the authority where you live. The authority responsible for me is the Bavarian Data Protection Authority (Bayerisches Landesamt für Datenschutzaufsicht, BayLDA) in Ansbach.

### Your right to object {#objection}

**All processing described in this policy is based on my legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can object to it at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation (Art. 21 GDPR). I will then stop processing your data, unless there are compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, or the processing is needed to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.**
